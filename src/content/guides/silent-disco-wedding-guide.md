Silent disco weddings started as a workaround for noise curfews. They stuck around because they are more fun than a regular reception. Here is how couples on the Gulf Coast usually run one.

## The timeline that works

Most receptions run in two acts.

**Act one, speakers.** Cocktail hour, dinner, toasts, first dance, parent dances. A regular DJ or playlist through speakers. Everyone hears the toasts. Nothing changes.

**Act two, headphones.** When the floor opens, headphones go out. This is usually 8:30 or 9 and runs until the end. If your venue has a 10 p.m. sound curfew, the switch happens right before it and nobody notices the music stopping, because it didn't.

Some couples go headphones from the first dance onward. It works, but toasts through headphones are awkward. We recommend speakers through the formalities.

## Programming three channels

Think in crowds, not genres.

- **Channel one, yours.** The playlist you and your partner built. This is the default channel and most guests stay on it.
- **Channel two, parents and grandparents.** Motown, 70s, 80s, classic country. This channel is the reason silent discos win over regular DJs. Your grandmother will dance.
- **Channel three, the friend group.** Whatever your college friends would request. Hip hop, EDM, 2000s throwbacks.

If you book our [DJ package](/rentals/#dj), the DJ programs all three and adjusts live. If you're renting headphones only, build three playlists on your phone or ask your DJ to run them.

## The switch moment

Announce it. Have the DJ or MC explain the channels and colors in one sentence. Hand out headphones from a table near the floor, or have the wedding party grab them first. Once the first ten people are dancing in silence, the rest follow within a song.

## Things people forget

- **Photos.** LED headphones photograph beautifully. Tell your photographer to plan for it.
- **Last dance.** Pick a song and put it on all three channels so everyone ends together.
- **Cocktail hour crowd.** Keep a few headphones at the bar. Guests who "don't dance" will try one.
- **Collection.** Put a bin by the exit and have the MC mention it. We count everything at the end.

## Venues along the Gulf Coast

Beach houses in Gulf Shores and Orange Beach, waterfront lawns in Fairhope and Point Clear, and the courtyards of downtown Mobile all share one thing: a noise ordinance. Silent disco is the standard fix. Ask your venue coordinator. Most have seen one already.
